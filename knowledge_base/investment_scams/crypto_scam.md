# Crypto and trading scams

Crypto scams are a fast-growing subset of investment fraud, exploiting the novelty of blockchains, the language of technology, and the absence of regulation in many jurisdictions.

## Fake trading platforms

Scammers build impressive-looking trading apps from templates, offer demo accounts showing gorgeous profits, and ask users to deposit real money (usually in crypto or via UPI). The dashboard shows growing balance until the withdrawal attempt, when the platform asks for a 'withdrawal fee', 'tax', or 'minimum balance' - and then vanishes.

## Signals of a fake platform

- No verifiable registration or license; claims like 'regulated in the Bahamas' without links to regulator databases.
- Withdrawals always blocked behind additional fees.
- Customer support only via Telegram/WhatsApp, no office, no phone number.
- Domain age is weeks; the domain is often on a cheap TLD.

## The 'signals guru' scam

WhatsApp/Telegram groups broadcast 'winning signals' and show screenshots of withdrawals. The group is mostly bots and fake accounts. The signals work just long enough to convince members to pay premium membership or copy real money into the scheme.

## The recovery scam

After an investment loss, 'recovery services' contact the victim claiming they can get the money back for an upfront fee. They are the same criminals, monetizing the victim twice. Anyone offering to recover crypto for a fee is by default part of another scam.

## Detection notes

Crypto vocabulary combined with 'guaranteed returns' and a registration fee receives the highest risk weighting in TextShield. The explanation layer notes that legitimate crypto trading offers no guarantees and no one collects fees to release withdrawals.

# Urgency tactics in scams

Urgency is the single most common manipulation device in spam and phishing. It works because it short-circuits the victim's reasoning: when something must be done "immediately", there is no time to verify.

## Why scammers use urgency

1. It motivates action before a victim can consult a family member, bank or professional.
2. It raises emotional stress, which lowers suspicion.
3. It justifies abnormal requests ("the offer expires in 1 hour, pay the fee now").
4. It prevents the discovery of the scam, which usually happens after a delay.

## Urgency phrases commonly found

- "Act now", "act immediately", "hurry", "right away"
- "Limited time offer", "offer expires today", "only 2 hours left"
- "Last chance", "final warning", "final notice", "don't delay"
- "Your account will be blocked/deleted/suspended in 24 hours"
- "Immediate action required", "respond within 1 hour"

## Legitimate urgency exists too

Banks genuinely remind customers about deadlines; flights genuinely remind about check-in. The difference: legitimate urgency never demands credentials, payments to strangers, or clicks on unverified links. An urgent message from an organization you deal with should always be verified through the organization's official app or website, never through the message itself.

## Detection note

TextShield flags urgency as a high-severity indicator because it appears in most scams, but one urgent phrase alone does not make a message spam. Urgency combined with financial requests, links or prize claims is what the risk engine considers dangerous.

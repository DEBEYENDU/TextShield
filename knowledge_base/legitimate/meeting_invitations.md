Title: Meeting Invitations
Category: legitimate
Subcategory: communication
Summary: Official meeting invitations from businesses, organizations, or colleagues.
Description: Legitimate meeting invitations contain verifiable meeting details (date, time, location, agenda) and come from recognized senders. These may be calendar invites, email invitations, or messaging platform requests. Legitimate invitations allow recipients to accept/decline and provide meeting access information.
Typical Scenario: "Project kickoff meeting - March 15, 10 AM" or "Team sync - weekly recurring" or "Client presentation - conference room B" or "Virtual meeting link for Thursday standup"
Intent: Coordinate schedules and facilitate communication for planned meetings
Behavior: Recipient receives genuine meeting invitation; can accept, decline, or propose alternative
Manipulation Techniques: None (legitimate communication serves organizational purpose)
Common Indicators: Verifiable meeting details; recognized sender or organization; calendar invite format; clear agenda or purpose; allows response/RSVP
Legitimate Alternatives: None - this is the legitimate communication itself
False Positives: Legitimate meeting invitations from various organizations the recipient is part of
False Negatives: Recipients who miss important meetings or deadlines
Real-world Examples: "Calendar invite from colleague," "Conference registration confirmation," "Team standup reminder," "Client meeting invitation"
References: Business communication best practices; Calendar platform guidelines (Google Calendar, Outlook); FTC Business Communication Guidelines

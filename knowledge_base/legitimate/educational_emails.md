Title: Educational Emails
Category: legitimate
Subcategory: communication
Summary: Legitimate educational and informational emails from schools, training providers, or educational organizations.
Description: Educational emails provide course information, learning resources, academic updates, or research findings from accredited institutions. These communications come from official educational domains and offer genuine learning opportunities or information.
Typical Scenario: "New lecture materials available" or "Registration open for Fall semester" or "Research paper: Recent findings in AI" or "Parent-teacher conference notice"
Intent: Inform and educate recipients about learning opportunities or academic matters
Behavior: Recipient receives genuine educational content or updates
Manipulation Techniques: None (legitimate communication provides educational value)
Common Indicators: Comes from official .edu or educational domain; relates to actual enrollment/coursework; no request for payment to access; provides genuine learning value
Legitimate Alternatives: None - this is the legitimate communication itself
False Positives: Legitimate educational emails that recipients didn't sign up for (e.g., conference announcements)
False Negatives: Students who miss important academic deadlines or opportunities
Real-world Examples: "Syllabus update" from professor, "Scholarship alert" from financial aid office, "Course registration" notification, "Library resource alert"
References: Accredited Educational Guidelines; University Communication Policies; FTC Educational Scam Prevention

# Delivery and parcel scams

Delivery scams exploit the flood of genuine courier notifications everyone receives. The scam message looks like a standard tracking alert but adds a payment demand or data request.

## Typical delivery scam templates

- "Your parcel is stuck at the sorting center. Pay Rs.35 redelivery fee via this link."
- "Your package is held at customs. Clear the customs charge of Rs.250 now."
- "Delivery failed. Update your address to reschedule."
- "Your order was undelivered. Confirm your card details to refund the amount."
- "DHL/FedEx/India Post: your shipment awaits clearance."

## The tell-tale details

- Real courier companies never demand customs or redelivery payments by SMS link.
- The "small amount" (Rs.35-500) is chosen to be not worth disputing - victims pay without thinking.
- The URL usually points to a fake courier-branded page harvesting cards.
- There may be no actual parcel at all: the scammers invent one.

## Why small-amount scams are dangerous

The psychological threshold for paying Rs.35 or Rs.99 is near zero, yet the same message reaches thousands, making delivery scams extremely profitable for criminals. The cumulative damage is real, and the phishing pages are indistinguishable from real courier sites.

## Detection notes

TextShield's indicator engine flags parcel/delivery patterns (medium severity), and combining this with a payment request and an unknown link escalates the risk to HIGH. The safest response: open the courier's official app or website and check the tracking number there, never pay through a link in an SMS.

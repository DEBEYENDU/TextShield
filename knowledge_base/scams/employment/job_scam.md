Title: Job Scam
Category: scams
Subcategory: employment
Summary: Fraudulent job offers aiming to extract money or personal information from job seekers.
Description: Job scams advertise attractive positions (remote work, high salary, minimal experience) to trick victims into paying for "equipment," "training," "background checks," or "visa processing." Some also collect personal information for identity theft. Legitimate employers never ask candidates to pay for hiring.
Typical Scenario: "Work from home - $50/hour - pay $150 for starter kit" or "Your application selected - pay $50 for background check clearance."
Intent: Financial gain and personal information theft
Behavior: Victim pays fees or reveals sensitive personal details
Manipulation Techniques: Greed (high income), Authority (claimed company), Urgency (application expires soon), Greed (work-from-home appeal)
Common Indicators: Job offer without interview; fee required for hiring; poor website quality; generic email addresses (Gmail/Yahoo) for "company"; request for bank details early in process
Legitimate Alternatives: Job applications through company career pages; legitimate recruitment agencies; standard hiring processes with no fees to candidate
False Positives: Legitimate companies with legitimate pre-employment checks (usually paid by employer, not candidate)
False Negatives: Job seekers discouraged from legitimate remote opportunities
Real-world Examples: "Virtual assistant scam," "Data entry pyramid schemes," "Reshipping mule recruitment"
References: FTC Job Scam Reports; Better Business Bureau Employment Scam Data; IC3 Internet Crime Report

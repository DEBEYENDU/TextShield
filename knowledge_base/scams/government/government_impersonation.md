Title: Government Impersonation
Category: scams
Subcategory: government
Summary: Scammers pose as government officials to extract money or personal information through fear and authority.
Description: Government impersonation scams involve contacting victims claiming to represent tax agencies, law enforcement, or government benefit offices. Victims are threatened with arrest, license suspension, or benefit cancellation unless they pay fines, taxes, or fees. Communications often appear official with fake badge numbers, letterheads, and case details.
Typical Scenario: "This is the IRS. You owe back taxes. Pay $2,000 immediately or face arrest warrant."
Intent: Financial extraction through fear of legal consequences
Behavior: Victim pays requested fees or reveals personal information
Manipulation Techniques: Authority (government agency), Fear (arrest/prosecution), Urgency (immediate payment demanded)
Common Indicators: Threat of arrest or legal action; demand for payment via gift cards/wire transfer; fake badge numbers; unsolicited contact about benefits/taxes
Legitimate Alternatives: Government agencies communicate via official channels; never demand immediate payment via untraceable methods
False Positives: Actual government debts or legal matters that are genuine
False Negatives: Victims who fall for sophisticated impersonation tactics
Real-world Examples: "IRS tax scam," "Social Security suspension" calls, "Jury duty missed" fraud
References: FTC Government Impersonation Reports; IRS Tax Scam Alerts

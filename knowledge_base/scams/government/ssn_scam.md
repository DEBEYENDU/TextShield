Title: Government Impersonation (Social Security)
Category: scams
Subcategory: government
Summary: Scammers impersonate Social Security Administration officials to extract personal information or money.
Description: Social Security scams involve calls or letters claiming the victim's SSN has been suspended, compromised, or involved in criminal activity. Victims are threatened with benefit suspension or arrest unless they pay a fee, provide personal information, or transfer money to "protect" their SSN. The SSA never suspends SSNs or demands payment over the phone.
Typical Scenario: "Your Social Security number has been suspended due to suspicious activity. Pay $100 to reactivate or face arrest."
Intent: Financial extraction and identity theft through SSN fear
Behavior: Victim pays fee or reveals SSN and personal details to fraudster
Manipulation Techniques: Fear (benefit loss/criminal charges), Authority (claimed SSA), Urgency (immediate action required)
Common Indicators: Threat of SSN suspension; demand for payment via gift cards/wire transfer; requests for full SSN; unsolicited contact; official-looking letterhead/number spoofing
Legitimate Alternatives: SSA communications via official mail; my.ssa.gov portal; SSA never suspends numbers over phone or demands payment
False Positives: Actual SSA communications about benefit reviews (sent by mail)
False Negatives: Beneficiaries who miss genuine SSA correspondence due to fear of scams
Real-world Examples: "SSN suspended" calls, "Benefit reinstatement" fraud, "Criminal activity investigation" extortion
References: SSA Scam Alerts; FTC Government Impersonation Reports; Social Security Administration Guidelines

Title: Government Impersonation (Utility)
Category: scams
Subcategory: government
Summary: Fraudulent communications posing as utility company representatives to extract payment or information.
Description: Utility scams involve calls, emails, or in-person visits claiming the victim's electricity, water, or gas service will be disconnected immediately unless payment is made. Scammers often threaten late fees, service termination, or legal action. They typically demand immediate payment via wire transfer, prepaid cards, or cryptocurrency.
Typical Scenario: "Your electricity will be shut off in 1 hour - pay $250 immediately via wire transfer to avoid disconnection."
Intent: Financial extraction through utility service threat
Behavior: Victim pays demanded fee; service continues normally after payment
Manipulation Techniques: Fear (service disruption), Authority (utility company), Urgency (immediate disconnection)
Common Indicators: Threat of immediate service cutoff; demand for unconventional payment methods; unsolicited contact; pressure to pay on spot; no prior notice via bill
Legitimate Alternatives: Utility companies send written notices; offer payment plans; never demand immediate payment via wire/gift cards; have official payment channels
False Positives: Actual overdue accounts that receive genuine disconnection notices
False Negatives: Customers who pay scammers fearing real disconnection
Real-world Examples: "Electricity disconnection" calls, "Water bill outstanding" extortion, "Gas leak emergency" scams
References: FTC Utility Scam Reports; National Association of Regulatory Utility Commissioners; State Public Utility Commissions

Title: Business Email Compromise
Category: scams
Subcategory: banking
Summary: Business Email Compromise (BEC) scams targeting organizations and employees.
Description: BEC scams involve fraudulent emails appearing to come from company executives, vendors, or partners requesting urgent wire transfers, invoice payments, or sensitive employee information. The emails often spoof the sender address and use social engineering to appear legitimate. Variants include CEO fraud, vendor email compromise, and attorney impersonation.
Typical Scenario: Email "from CEO" to accounting: "Urgent wire transfer of $50,000 to close this deal - discretely" or "Invoice payment change - new banking details for vendor X"
Intent: Financial gain through business deception
Behavior: Victim initiates wire transfer or data disclosure to fraudster
Manipulation Techniques: Authority (claimed executive/partner), Urgency (time-sensitive deal/invoice), Trust Building (known business context), Scarcity (limited-time offer)
Common Indicators: Request for wire transfer or gift cards; changed payment details; email from slightly altered address; secrecy request; sense of emergency
Legitimate Alternatives: Verified payment channels; direct phone confirmation of payment changes; established vendor verification processes; multi-person approval for transfers
False Positives: Actual legitimate business requests that happen to be time-sensitive
False Negatives: Employees unaware of BEC tactics or lacking verification protocols
Real-world Examples: "CEO wire transfer fraud," "Vendor invoice change scam," "Attorney impersonation fraud"

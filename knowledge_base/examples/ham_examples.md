# Example ham messages (legitimate messages for retrieval)

These representative legitimate messages prove that spam-like signals sometimes appear in innocuous contexts. Retrieval of similar HAM examples helps the explainer calibrate: a message resembling these should be interpreted carefully even if it contains a link or a transaction.

- "Hey, are we meeting at 5 PM today at the library?"
- "Mom said dinner is ready. Can you come home early?"
- "Your order from Amazon has been delivered. Thank you for shopping with us."
- "Dear student, tomorrow's class is postponed to 3 PM."
- "Your electricity bill of Rs.1,240 is due on 25th. Please pay on time."
- "Train 12345 from Delhi is delayed by 30 minutes."
- "I am on my way. Will reach in 10 minutes."
- "Your recharge of Rs.299 was successful. New balance: Rs.512."
- "The payment of Rs.1,500 has been credited to your account."
- "Your taxi is arriving in 5 minutes. Please wait at the main gate."
- "Doctor appointment confirmed for Tuesday, 10:00 AM."
- "Your internship stipend of Rs.8,000 has been transferred."
- "Your exam result has been announced. Check the notice board."
- "The project meeting has been rescheduled to Friday at 11 AM."
- "Please find attached the assignment document. Submit it by Monday."
- "Happy birthday! Wishing you a wonderful year ahead."
- "Semester exams start from 1st December. Get your hall tickets."
- "Your metro card recharge is successful."
- "Your library fine is Rs.10. Please clear it soon."
- "Your guest wifi access has been revoked due to policy change."

Key point: legitimate transactional messages (bill reminders, payment confirmations, delivery updates) frequently carry urgency or financial vocabulary. The difference from spam is consistency: a known sender, a verifiable source, no links to unknown domains, and no requests to share credentials or pay strangers.

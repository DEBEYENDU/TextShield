Spam: Unsolicited, usually bulk, messages sent via email, SMS, or other communication channels, often advertising products, services, or content without recipient consent. Spam may be commercial, political, or malicious in nature, and typically violates acceptable use policies of communication platforms.

Ham: Legitimate, desired, or expected communication that is not spam. Ham includes personal messages, legitimate business communications, transactional updates, and other expected correspondence that recipients have consented to or anticipate receiving.

Phishing: Fraudulent communication (typically email, but also SMS, phone, or fake websites) that impersonates legitimate entities to trick recipients into revealing sensitive information such as credentials, financial details, or personal information. Phishing often creates urgency, fear, or authority to bypass critical thinking.

Smishing: SMS phishing - phishing attempts delivered via text messages to mobile devices. Smishing typically includes suspicious links or requests for personal information under false pretenses (e.g., account issues, prize claims, delivery problems).

Vishing: Voice phishing - phishing attempts delivered via phone calls. Vishing scammers often impersonate government agencies, tech support, banks, or other legitimate entities to extract money, credentials, or personal information through social engineering over the phone.

Semantic Search: A search technique that understands the intent and contextual meaning of a query rather than matching exact keywords. Semantic search analyzes concepts, relationships, and context to return more relevant results, crucial for knowledge base retrieval where concepts may be described differently than exact terms.

Embedding: A numerical representation of text or data in a vector format that captures semantic meaning. Embeddings convert documents, sentences, or concepts into multi-dimensional vectors where similar meanings have similar vector representations, enabling comparison and retrieval without exact matching.

Vector Database: A specialized database designed to store and query vector embeddings efficiently. Vector databases enable fast similarity search and retrieval of related documents based on semantic meaning rather than exact keyword matches.

Chunking: The process of breaking down larger documents into smaller, manageable pieces (chunks) for processing, indexing, or embedding. Chunking ensures that relevant content segments are embedded together while maintaining contextual integrity, typically involving overlap between consecutive chunks to preserve meaning.

Intent: The purpose or goal behind a communication pattern or behavior. Intent categorizes whether a communication is designed to inform, persuade, manipulate, entertain, or achieve another purpose, and is central to distinguishing legitimate from malicious communication.

Behavior: The observable actions or responses associated with a particular pattern or communication type. Behavior describes what recipients do in response to the communication (e.g., click a link, provide information, make a payment, ignore the message) and what actions the communication encourages.

Entity Extraction: The process of identifying and extracting specific pieces of information (entities) from text, such as names, dates, amounts, addresses, phone numbers, organizations, or other categorized data. Entity extraction is crucial for identifying key details in both legitimate and scam communications.

RAG: Retrieval-Augmented Generation - a system that retrieves relevant knowledge from a knowledge base and provides it as context to an LLM to generate more accurate, grounded responses. RAG combines information retrieval with LLM generation to enhance accuracy and reduce hallucination.

LLM: Large Language Model - a type of artificial intelligence trained on vast amounts of text data to understand and generate human-like language. LLMs can process, analyze, and generate text based on patterns and information in their training data, and are used for tasks including summarization, analysis, and generation.

Hallucination: When an LLM generates information that is false, fabricated, or not supported by its training data or provided context. Hallucinations occur when the model confidently presents incorrect information, which is particularly risky in cybersecurity and knowledge contexts where accuracy is critical.

Prompt Injection: A technique where malicious or carefully crafted inputs are designed to override or manipulate an LLM's behavior, causing it to disregard original instructions or produce unintended outputs. Prompt injection can extract sensitive information, bypass safety measures, or cause the model to act against its intended purpose.

Confidence: A numerical score (typically 0.0 to 1.0) indicating the degree of certainty or reliability associated with a classification, prediction, or assessment. Confidence scores help prioritize and filter knowledge base entries, search results, or classifications based on reliability.

Risk: The potential for loss, damage, or negative consequence resulting from a particular communication pattern, behavior, or decision. Risk assessment evaluates the likelihood and impact of harmful outcomes from engaging with scam communications, implementing security measures, or making decisions based on knowledge base information.

Title: Tech Support Scams
Category: social_engineering
Summary: Fraudsters pose as Microsoft, Apple, or bank technicians, fabricate device threats, and demand remote access or payment.
Description: Tech support scams start with a fake alert (virus, breach, expired license) and escalate to remote-access tools and gift-card or transfer payments. Legitimate support never cold-calls about infections, never demands remote access to strangers' devices, and never takes gift cards.
Typical Scenario: "Virus detected, install TeamViewer" or "Your license expired, call now" or "Refund requires remote access"
Purpose: Recognize support impersonation and remote-access traps
Detection Signals: unsolicited infection claim; remote-access demand; gift-card or crypto payment; refusal to use official support channels
Legitimate Uses: User-initiated support sessions through official apps with case IDs
Examples: "Fake Microsoft pop-up," "AnyDesk refund scam," "Antivirus renewal fraud"
Risk Level: High - combines fear, authority, and remote control
References: FTC Tech Support Reports; Microsoft Digital Crimes Unit

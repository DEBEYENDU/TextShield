Title: Authority Bias
Category: psychology
Summary: People obey perceived authority figures without verifying credentials, a bias attackers exploit through impersonation.
Description: Authority bias makes recipients comply with requests from anyone presenting as an official, executive, or expert. Attackers borrow titles, letterheads, and institutional language to inherit trust they never earned. Defense is independent verification through a separate channel.
Typical Scenario: "RBI directs all customers" or "CEO instructs urgent transfer" or "Cyber cell officer calling"
Purpose: Explain why impersonation works; calibrate trust toward claimed authority
Detection Signals: title without verifiable identity; orders instead of requests; secrecy demands; refusal of callbacks; mismatched sender domain
Legitimate Uses: Real regulators and executives communicating through verified channels with reference numbers
Examples: "Fake RBI KYC order," "CEO fraud wire transfer," "Bogus income-tax officer call"
Risk Level: High - obedience to authority is near-universal
References: Milgram Obedience Studies; FTC Impersonation Data; Cialdini Influence

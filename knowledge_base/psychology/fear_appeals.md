Title: Fear Appeals in Fraud
Category: psychology
Summary: Attackers manufacture fear of loss, punishment, or exposure to force hasty compliance.
Description: Fear appeals describe a threat (blocked account, arrest, data leak) and offer a single escape route controlled by the attacker. Fear narrows attention and suppresses verification. Legitimate warnings instead give time, reference numbers, and independent contact paths.
Typical Scenario: "Account will be blocked in 2 hours" or "Arrest warrant issued" or "Virus detected, call now"
Purpose: Distinguish manufactured panic from genuine alerts
Detection Signals: catastrophic consequence; tiny compliance window; single attacker-controlled remedy; no reference number; threats of secrecy
Legitimate Uses: Genuine security alerts with verification paths and reasonable timelines
Examples: "Fake account suspension SMS," "Digital arrest scam," "Bogus virus warning"
Risk Level: High - fear overrides rational checks
References: FTC Fear-Based Fraud Reports; CERT-In Advisories

# Safety guidelines: what to do if you were scammed

If you already paid money, shared an OTP, or clicked a phishing link, act quickly - speed matters, and calm matters more. This document is informational guidance, not professional legal or financial advice.

## Immediate steps

1. **Stop all contact** - do not answer further calls, do not pay any additional 'fees', do not argue with the scammer. Every further interaction is another attempt.
2. **Call your bank immediately** - report the unauthorized transfer or shared credentials. Banks can place freezes and, in many countries, initiate chargebacks/refund tracing.
3. **Change passwords everywhere** - starting with email (the master key) and payment apps. Use a different device if the current one is compromised.
4. **Enable 2FA on accounts that had their data exposed** - and check for unauthorized devices/sessions in account settings.
5. **Report the fraudulent number/account**:
   - India: cyber crime helpline 1930 and https://cybercrime.gov.in - report frauduable UPI/account numbers so they can be frozen.
   - Report the scam message to the impersonated brand's official helpline as well.
6. **Monitor** - watch bank statements and credit reports for the following weeks; identity theft often surfaces late.

## What to keep

Screenshots of the message, the number, the URL (but do not click it again), and the payment reference numbers. These become evidence for the complaint.

## Report of the report

Complaints on the national cyber portal are typically received quickly. Save the complaint ID. Losing the receipts is what makes recovery paperwork impossible.

## Emotional note

Response scams are common after fraud: 'recovery agents' contact victims promising to retrieve money for a fee. They are the same criminals. Never pay anyone to recover money; only official channels (bank, regulator, police) process restitution.

## Tell someone

Fraud thrives on secrecy. Telling family and friends removes the shame layer and protects others from the same message. Most scam campaigns hit entire address books indirectly, and a forwarded screenshot can save someone else.

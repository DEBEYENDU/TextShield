# Safety guidelines: protecting yourself

These guidelines are general, informational advice. They are not legal, medical or financial authority, and TextShield does not claim to provide any of those.

## The ten defensive habits

1. **No OTP, ever** - no bank, government office or company will legitimately ask for your OTP. Anyone who asks is a fraudster.
2. **No credentials by message** - never type passwords, PINs, MPINs or CVV into pages reached from unsolicited SMS/email links.
3. **Verify through official channels** - for any account alert, open the official app or website yourself instead of using the link or number in the message.
4. **Look before you click** - hover over links to inspect the real destination; check the domain spelling character by character (paypa1.com, amaz0n-offers.xyz).
5. **Never pay to receive** - prizes, loans, jobs and refunds never require advance fees. Money flows to you, not from you.
6. **Keep software updated** - updated OS, browser and apps fix the security holes that phishing pages and malicious attachments exploit.
7. **Unique passwords + 2FA** - a leaked password cannot gate other accounts, and 2FA (non-SMS, e.g. authenticator app) blocks most account takeovers.
8. **Limit personal data sharing** - job 'registration forms' and KYC-pretending pages collect documents used later for identity theft.
9. **Check the sender of emails** - the display name can be anything; only the real domain matters and lookalike domains are one character away.
10. **Pause under pressure** - urgency is the scammer's tool. When a message demands speed, slow down and verify.

## When a message passes your checks

If the sender is known, there are no links to unknown domains, nothing requests credentials, and the content matches normal communication, the message is most likely legitimate. Even then, prefer the official channel for anything financial.

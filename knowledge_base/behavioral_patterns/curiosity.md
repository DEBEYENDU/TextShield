Title: Curiosity
Category: behavioral_patterns
Summary: A manipulation technique that exploits the human desire for new information or resolution of uncertainty.
Description: Curiosity-based manipulation piques interest through intriguing statements, mysterious content, or unresolved scenarios that prompt victims to click, read, or act to satisfy their curiosity. Scammers use intriguing subject lines, mysterious messages, or "you won't believe this" formats to drive engagement. Legitimate uses include genuine marketing, educational content, and informative communications.
Typical Scenario: "You've been mentioned in this" or "Someone is searching for you" or "This one thing will change your life" or "Mysterious package delivered to your name"
Purpose: Exploit information gap; drive engagement; prompt action to resolve curiosity gap
Detection Signals: Intriguing but vague statements; "you won't believe"; mysterious context; promises of sensational information; unclear sender or context; curiosity gap without legitimate source
Legitimate Uses: Genuine marketing subject lines; educational content teasers; informative announcements; newsletter previews
Examples: "This changed everything" "Breaking news you need to know" "Someone is talking about you" "Mysterious find at your address"
Risk Level: Medium - curiosity effective but often recognizable
References: FTC Curiosity-Based Scam Reports; Information gap psychology; APWG Curiosity in Phishing Subject Lines

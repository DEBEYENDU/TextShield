Title: Authority
Category: behavioral_patterns
Summary: A manipulation technique that exploits deference to perceived authority figures or institutions.
Description: Authority scams impersonate legitimate organizations, officials, or figures to compel compliance. Victims are more likely to follow requests from perceived authority without question. Scammers use titles, fake credentials, spoofed uniforms, or official-looking documentation to establish false authority.
Typical Scenario: "This is the IRS/ police/ your bank - you must comply immediately" or "Your supervisor has issued this requirement" or "Government agency requires this verification"
Purpose: Establish legitimacy; compel compliance through perceived power; bypass normal skepticism
Detection Signals: unsolicited contact claiming authority; demands without explanation; threats of consequences for non-compliance; official-looking but unverifiable credentials; refusal to provide independent verification
Legitimate Uses: Actual authority figures in legitimate contexts; organizational hierarchies; regulatory compliance; verified official communications
Examples: "IRS calling about taxes," "Manager email requesting gift cards," "Bank security alert," "Police threat of arrest"
Risk Level: High - authority exploitation is highly effective across demographics
References: FTC Authority Impersonation Reports; Milgram Experiment on Obedience; APWG Authority Phishing Data
